Abstract

Humanoid robots with quasi-direct-drive actuators continuously generate joint torque while standing, whereas seated humans delegate weight support to chairs during desk work. As a first step toward seated loco-manipulation, we study omnidirectional seated locomotion on a passive mobile chair with casters, a robot–chair–floor system that requires unfixed pelvis–seat contact and intermittent foot–floor propulsion of the robot and chair. We ask whether this task can be learned by minimally extending a standard standing velocity-tracking environment, adding only a passive-chair model, seated-state rewards, critic-only chair observations, and pelvis–seat contact settings. The policy is learned without motion references, and its actor uses only proprioception and velocity commands. In random-command evaluation, the policies tracked omnidirectional commands through nearly all 20-s rollouts with accuracy comparable to a standing locomotion policy. Comparing symmetry regularization (SY), foot-slip penalty (FS), and command curriculum (CC) showed that FS reduced the cost of transport (CoT) but increased tracking error, and that some FS-only policies converged to stationary local optima under diagonal-forward commands. Adding either SY or CC avoided these local optima. CC first learns low-speed propulsion, where smaller foot forces suffice to propel without slipping, and then expands the command range. SY further reduced the left–right bias in leg use that otherwise persisted, likely because the chair supports the body. Direction-resolved analysis showed CoT ordered backward < lateral ≪ forward, with planted-leg extension in backward and lateral motion and knee flexion following heel contact in forward motion. The learned policy achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Unitree G1.

Method

Overview of the seated locomotion learning framework, including the asymmetric actor–critic architecture, refined pelvis collision mesh, and passive-chair model. The full-factorial study varies command curriculum (CC), symmetry regularization (SY), and foot-slip penalty (FS).
